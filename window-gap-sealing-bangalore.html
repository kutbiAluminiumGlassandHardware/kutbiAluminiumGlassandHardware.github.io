Signs Your Window Gap Needs Repair

  • A visible gap remains when the window is fully closed.
  • The gap is larger on one side than the other.
  • Outside air enters around the shutter or frame.
  • Rainwater enters around a visible opening.
  • Rubber beading looks loose, hard or damaged.
  • The sliding shutter does not sit evenly after closing.
  • Temporary sealing has not solved the recurring gap.

A gap is a symptom rather than a diagnosis. Kutbi checks sealing, alignment, closing action and related sliding components to identify the appropriate repair.

Window Gap Sealing Repair Process

  1. Identify where the gap is visible and whether it changes when the window moves.
  2. Check the shutter and frame for alignment and closing position.
  3. Inspect rubber beading and other visible sealing conditions.
  4. Check wheels, rollers and tracks on sliding systems where relevant.
  5. Determine whether the gap needs sealing, adjustment, beading replacement or another related repair.
  6. Complete the suitable repair and test the window's closing and sealing.

Window Gap Sealing Repair FAQs

Why is there a gap around my closed window?

Possible causes include worn beading, poor alignment, shutter position, hardware or sliding-system problems.

Can rubber beading cause a window gap?

Yes. Deteriorated or loose beading can reduce the seal and leave visible openings.

Can sealing alone fix a sliding window gap?

Not always. If rollers, tracks or alignment are causing the shutter to sit incorrectly, the underlying issue may need repair first.

Can a window gap cause air leakage?

Yes. An opening around a closed window can allow outside air to enter.

Can a window gap cause water leakage?

It can contribute to rainwater entry, depending on the location and condition of the window system.

Can I send a photo first?

Yes. A clear photo showing the complete window and the visible gap can help with an initial assessment.
