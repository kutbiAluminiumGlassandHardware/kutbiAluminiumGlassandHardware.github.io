Signs Your Window May Have an Air Leakage Problem

  • Outside air can be felt around a closed window.
  • A visible gap remains around the shutter or frame.
  • The window closes unevenly or requires force.
  • Rubber beading appears hard, loose or damaged.
  • A sliding shutter does not sit correctly after closing.
  • Air leakage continues after a temporary sealing attempt.

The point where air is felt is not always the root cause. Kutbi checks the window's seals, gaps, alignment, closing action and related sliding components.

Window Air Leakage Repair Process

  1. Identify where outside air enters and whether the problem changes with the window position.
  2. Inspect the frame, shutter and visible gaps.
  3. Check rubber beading and sealing condition.
  4. Check alignment and whether the window closes correctly.
  5. Inspect tracks, wheels and rollers where applicable.
  6. Carry out suitable adjustment, sealing, beading replacement or related repair and test the window.

Window Air Leakage Repair FAQs

Why is air coming through my closed window?

Possible causes include worn seals or beading, gaps, alignment issues, incomplete closing and sliding-system problems.

Can rubber beading stop window air leakage?

If deteriorated beading is the cause, suitable beading replacement can restore the seal around the window.

Can a sliding window be repaired if it lets air in?

Suitable existing sliding windows can be assessed for alignment, rollers, tracks, seals and closing problems.

Should I seal the gap without checking the window?

Not always. If alignment, closing or component wear is causing the gap, sealing alone may not solve the underlying problem.

Can aluminium windows have air leakage?

Yes. Air leakage can occur when sealing, gaps, alignment or closing conditions are not working correctly.

Can I send a photo first?

Yes. A clear photo of the complete window and the area where air enters can help with an initial assessment.
