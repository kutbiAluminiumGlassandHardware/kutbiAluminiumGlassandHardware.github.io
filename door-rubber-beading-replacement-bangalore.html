Common Door Beading & Seal Problems

Worn or hardened beading

Old sealing material can become hard, brittle, loose or less effective over time.

Loose or damaged seal

Loose sections or visible damage can contribute to gaps, drafts or water entry.

Glass-fit related sealing

Where the door contains suitable existing glass, Kutbi can assess the glass, beading and surrounding frame together.

Alignment-related gaps

A door that is out of alignment may not seal correctly even after beading replacement.

Door Beading Replacement: Repair-First Assessment

Kutbi checks whether the existing aluminium or uPVC door and frame remain suitable. Where the system is suitable, affected compatible rubber beading or seal material can be replaced and the door can then be checked for fit, closing and sealing.

  1. Inspect visible beading and seal condition.
  2. Check the door frame and glass-fit condition where relevant.
  3. Check alignment and closing position.
  4. Identify whether leakage or gaps are also present.
  5. Replace suitable compatible beading or seal material where appropriate.
  6. Test closing, fit and sealing after the work.

Door Rubber Beading FAQs

When should door rubber beading be replaced?

Replacement may be appropriate when compatible beading is worn, hardened, damaged or no longer seals correctly.

Will new beading fix a leaking door?

It may help when the seal is the cause, but leakage can also involve alignment, frame or other conditions. Kutbi checks the system first.

Does beading replacement mean the door must be replaced?

No. Where the existing door remains suitable, compatible beading or seal replacement may be enough.
