Signs Your Window May Need Water Leakage Repair

  • Rainwater enters around the window frame or shutter.
  • Water appears near the bottom or side of a sliding window.
  • Rubber beading looks hardened, loose or damaged.
  • A visible gap remains after the window is closed.
  • The shutter does not close or align evenly.
  • Water leakage returns after temporary sealing.

The visible leak is not always the actual cause. Checking the window's closing, alignment, seals and sliding components helps identify the appropriate repair.

Window Water Leakage Repair Process

  1. Identify where and when water enters the window.
  2. Inspect the shutter, frame and visible gaps.
  3. Check rubber beading and sealing condition.
  4. Check alignment and whether the window closes correctly.
  5. Inspect sliding tracks and related wheels or rollers where applicable.
  6. Carry out suitable repair, adjustment, beading replacement or related work and test the window.

Window Water Leakage Repair FAQs

Why is rainwater coming through my window?

Possible causes include worn seals or beading, gaps, poor closing, alignment issues and sliding-system conditions. The actual cause needs to be assessed.

Can rubber beading cause water leakage?

Yes. Deteriorated or loose beading can reduce sealing around the window system.

Can a sliding window be repaired if it leaks?

Suitable existing sliding windows can be assessed for the source of leakage and related operating problems.

Should I just apply sealant?

Sealant may not address the underlying problem if the window is misaligned, not closing correctly or has worn components.

Can water leakage happen with aluminium windows?

Yes. Aluminium window leakage can be related to sealing, gaps, alignment and operating conditions.

Can I send a photo first?

Yes. A clear photo of the complete window and the area where water enters can help with an initial assessment.
